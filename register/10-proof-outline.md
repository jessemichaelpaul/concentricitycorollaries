# The proof, end to end

Grounded in `Octonionic_RH_master.tex` and in the cited category theory
(`register/40-categorical-engine.md`). Where the settled register reads differently from the master,
the difference is recorded in §9 as a delta. **The master is not edited during the Lean endgame**;
the deltas are the record of what to reconcile afterwards.

## 1. The theorem

```lean
ASection.concentricity (A : ASection) :
  ∃ c : ℝ, ∀ n : ℕ, (A.sphereZero n).re = c
```

An A-section is a slice-preserving function on the compactified octonions `𝕆* = S⁸` satisfying
C1–C4. The Concentricity Theorem says its residue-`ℂ` zero 6-spheres are concentric. The octonionic
zeta is one A-section among many, and the Riemann Hypothesis is one of its corollaries
(master §Part 3 preamble, :815–821).

## 2. C1–C4 are inputs

- **C1** — meromorphic continuation through the real pole to the common north pole `N = ∞`.
- **C2** — the infinite Euler product, prime index internal.
- **C3** — the infinite Weierstrass product over the full divisor.
- **C4** — infinitude of the residue-`ℂ` zeros.

C4 makes the infinitely many zeros **intrinsic to the analytic object**, not a population installed
downstream. The realized zero population and its concentricity are outputs.

## 3. One distinguished element

C1–C3 determine a single distinguished element (master :1151–1156):

> the Euler product presents it at `0` and the Weierstrass product at `N`, the two boundary points it
> fixes, as a single diagonal Möbius element, through the commuting triangle `π∘E = exp`; per prime a
> centered degenerate family with one real level, each lifting uniquely to `N`.

The winding, lift-uniqueness, tameness, crossing, welding, and GPV transport laws are **readings of
that one action**, not twelve separate facts. The element is simultaneously a function in the
A-section ring and a Möbius group element; the green bridge is `eulerDiskAction_eq_value`
(`ProjectiveTransport.lean:218`) — the disk action's multiplier *is* `A.F z`.

Because the element is a function, its value states are inherited by evaluation. There is no
separate reader to install them.

## 4. Normalization is input to the element

For `s ∈ 𝕆*`, the normalization `(dir s, sliceCoord s)` selects the slice sphere and chart coordinate
**where the already-complete element is evaluated**:

```text
s ∈ 𝕆*  ↦  (dir s, sliceCoord s)  ↦  the sphere S²_(dir s) and its coordinate  ↦  A(s)
```

At real points and at `N` the slice direction is non-unique and irrelevant: those points are common
to every slice sphere and the realization is direction-blind there. This is why `N` is a shared
witness rather than a chosen one. *(Delta D1.)*

## 5. The global geometry

Orbit–stabilizer transports the same element through every projective frame, **objects and arrows
together**, and does so uniformly on every Riemann sphere — because the projective real circle and
the two fixed points `0`, `N` are common to all of them. `G₂` supplies the natural isomorphisms
between different sphere directions `I → J` in `SphereWorld`.

So orbit–stabilizer never has to manufacture the continuum of directions: the continuum is already
present, and `G₂` connects it. A slicewise proof is admissible only when it is uniform in an
arbitrary `I : SphereWorld` and immediately certified by `G₂` naturality. Choosing a `baseWorld` and
sweeping afterwards is a sectional projection, not the global action.

The identity and composition laws here are pure group algebra: every base arrow factors as
`f = orbitRep(Y) · stab(f) · orbitRep(X)⁻¹`, and `stabilizerPart_id` / `_comp` make the extension
functorial (master :1156–1158).

`AsectionSlice A : GreatCircle.Base ⥤ SphereWorld` is the **geometric projection** of this action
(master calls it `A^slice`, :1159). Its object map is constant by `rfl` —
`distinguishedWorldAction.obj I := I`, so `obj X = baseWorld` for every `X` — because the element
moves *inside* each Riemann sphere rather than relabelling the slice direction. Legitimate as a
projection; never the global action.

## 6. The derived groupoid-valued diagram

The `G₂`-equivariant realization lifts the same action to the normalized value-state fibres, giving

```text
F_A : 𝓑 ⥤ Grpd
```

over the projective great-circle base (master :1160–1163). `F_A(X)` is the groupoid of all intrinsic
value states generated by the one action positioned at `X`; `F_A.map f` is the invertible transport of
the whole element. Its arrows are the actual value transports — orbit–stabilizer in the
projective/Möbius direction, GPV in the prime/winding direction, `G₂` in the sphere direction, and
input/output naturality joining the domain and codomain readings.

The `⥤ Grpd` codomain is **required** by the literal statement of the components equivalence (§8).
What is forbidden is a generic instantiation that consumes no `A`.

**This is the open gate.** Its exact native Lean type is Gate 1's audit subject.

## 7. The total

```text
T_A = ∫_𝓑 F_A
```

An object remembers a projective footpoint and an action-generated value state; an arrow remembers a
base motion together with the induced value transport. The Grothendieck construction **records** the
already-unified function/Möbius action; it does not fuse two independently built natures
(master :1166).

## 8. The readout, in its settled order

Every type below is read from the completed action and its total, never predicted.

1. **Categorical zero preimage.** Read `F_A⁰(X) ⊆ F_A(X)`, the states whose canonical output is zero.
   Output naturality restricts all transports to these subgroupoids. This is the categorical inverse
   image of the completed output functor — not an independently populated zero diagram. Then
   `T_A⁰ = ∫ F_A⁰`.
2. **North restriction.** Read the canonical inclusion `K_A : C_A ⥤ D_A`, retaining the natural
   isomorphisms of the zero-state groupoid at `N` — not merely the base stabilizer. The zero/N cone
   is inherited from the same element: it fixes `0` and `N`, its GPV action carries the north
   transport, orbit–stabilizer conjugates it in every frame, `G₂` makes it natural across
   `SphereWorld`.
3. **Literal finality (Riehl Lemma 8.3.4).** Prove every slice `d/K_A` non-empty and connected. That,
   and only that, is Lemma 8.3.4.
4. **The chosen colimit class.** Apply finality to `X_A = π₀ ∘ F_A⁰`. The category-of-elements
   identity `π₀(el X) ≅ colim X` (a sub-fact inside 8.3.4's proof, book p. 102) together with Remark
   8.3.5 (non-empty + connected ⇔ `π₀` a singleton) yields the one zero-family class `κ_col`.
5. **The separate components equivalence.** Only now apply
   `pi0GrothendieckEquiv F_A⁰ : π₀(T_A⁰) ≃ colim(π₀ ∘ F_A⁰)` — an `≃`, a different theorem from
   8.3.4 — carrying `κ_col` to the class `κ` in the **zero** total, not in the unrestricted `T_A`.
6. **The reader.** A cone under the completed zero diagram induces `val`. It **reads** the register
   the class already carries; it never creates one. `c := val κ`, and the atomic conclusion follows.

The colimit consumes the **chosen** transport system. Never whole-total connectedness, never generic
zigzag invariance as the mechanism, never `Subsingleton (ConnectedComponents T_A)`.

## 10. Corollaries

With `c` in hand, every populated residue-`ℂ` zero 6-sphere has centre `c`, so the infinitely many
populated zero spheres are concentric. The theorem does not prescribe the numerical value of `c`
(master :1146–1148). The octonionic zeta is an A-section, so the Riemann Hypothesis follows, along
with the infinitude of critical-line zeros — `Corollaries.lean`.
