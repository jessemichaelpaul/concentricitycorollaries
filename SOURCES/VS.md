### Remark 5.2 (Section 5, "The hypercomplex logarithm and n-th root"; printed p. 988; ar5iv id `S5.Thmtheo2`)
> **Remark 5.2**
> (a) If $\pi : {\mathbb {K}}\times \mathrm{Im}({\mathbb {K}}) \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$ denotes the projection on the first factor, then by definition the following equality holds
> $$(\pi \circ E)(q)= \exp (q)$$
> for all $q\in {\mathbb {K}}$.
> (b) Unlike what happens in the complex setting, the map $\pi :{\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+ \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$ is not a covering. It is not an open map as well, due to the fact that $\exp :{\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$ is not an open map (it has a non–empty degenerate set consisting of spheres).

### Proposition 4.1 (Section 4, "Other examples of hypercomplex Riemann manifolds"; printed p. 983; ar5iv id `S4.Thmtheo1`)
> **Proposition 4.1**
> Let us set $m = \dim {\mathbb {K}}\in \{4, 8\}$. Consider the unit sphere $S^{m}\subset {\mathbb {R}}^{m+1}\cong {\mathbb {K}}\times {\mathbb {R}}$ and the inverse of the stereographic projection from the north pole $N=(0,\ldots ,0,1)$ of $S^{m}$ onto the equatorial plane ${\mathbb {K}}\cong {\mathbb {R}}^{m}$, namely
> $$f: {\mathbb {R}}^{m}\cong {\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow S^{m}\setminus \{N\} \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times {\mathbb {R}}\cong {\mathbb {R}}^{m+1}, \tag{4.6}$$
> defined by
> $$f(x+Iy)=\left( \frac{2(x+Iy)}{1+x^2+y^2}, \frac{-1+x^2+y^2}{1+x^2+y^2} \right). \tag{4.7}$$
> Then $S^{m}\setminus \{N\}$ is a special parameterized hypercomplex Riemann manifold and the map $f$ is a conformal parameterization.
> Analogous statement can be proved for the stereographic projection from the south pole $S.$

(Note the pole notation: $N$ is the north pole $(0,\ldots,0,1)$ of $S^m$ — the point the master's compactification sends to $\infty$; transport to $\mathbb{O}^* = S^8$ is the master's own marked derivation, per `rmk:compactify`.)

### Theorem 4.2 (Section 4; printed p. 984; ar5iv id `S4.Thmtheo2`)
> **Theorem 4.2**
> Let us set $m = \dim {\mathbb {K}}\in \{4, 8\}$. Let $f$ and $h$ be the following maps
> $$f: {\mathbb {R}}^m\cong {\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow S^m\setminus \{N\} \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times {\mathbb {R}}\cong {\mathbb {R}}^{m+1}$$
> $$f(x+Iy)=\left( \frac{2(x+Iy)}{1+x^2+y^2}, \frac{-1+x^2+y^2}{1+x^2+y^2} \right)$$
> and
> $$h: {\mathbb {R}}^m\cong {\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow S^m\setminus \{S\} \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times {\mathbb {R}}\cong {\mathbb {R}}^{m+1}$$
> $$h(x+Iy)=\left( \frac{2(x-Iy)}{1+x^2+y^2}, \frac{1-x^2-y^2}{1+x^2+y^2} \right).$$
> Then the differentiable conformal atlas $\left\{ ({\mathbb {K}}, f), ({\mathbb {K}}, h)\right\}$ endows $S^m\subset {\mathbb {R}}^{m+1}$ with a structure of slice quaternionic or slice octonionic manifold.

### Proposition 5.1 (Section 5; printed p. 988; ar5iv id `S5.Thmtheo1`)
> **Proposition 5.1**
> Let $f: {\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}\times \mathrm{Im}({\mathbb {K}})$ be the map defined by
> $$f(x+Iy)=(\sinh x \cos y +I\sinh x \sin y, Iy)$$
> for $I\in {\mathbb {S}}$, $x, y\in {\mathbb {R}}$. Let ${\mathbb {K}}^+=\{q\in {\mathbb {K}}: \mathrm{Re}\ q >0\}$, and set ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+:=f({\mathbb {K}}^+)$.
> The ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$-exponential map
> $$E : {\mathbb {K}}\rightarrow {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+ \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times \mathrm{Im}({\mathbb {K}})$$
> defined by:
> $$E(x+Iy) = (\exp (x + Iy), Iy) = (\exp x \cos y + I\exp x \sin y, Iy)$$
> is an immersion and a diffeomorphism between ${\mathbb {K}}$ and ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+.$ In the case of quaternions, it endows ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {H}}^+$ with a structure of slice quaternionic manifold (see, e.g., [4]), which is different from the structure of hypercomplex Riemann manifold defined in Proposition 4.3. However, this manifold will be denoted simply by ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$, and called the logarithm manifold.

### Definition 5.3 (Section 5; printed p. 989; ar5iv id `S5.Thmtheo3`)
> **Definition 5.3**
> Let ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$ be the logarithm manifold. The ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$-logarithm
> $$L : {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+ \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times \mathrm{Im}({\mathbb {K}}) \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$$
> is defined as follows, in terms of the real logarithm $\log$,
> $$L (q, p) := \log |q| + p,$$
> where $p$ is called the argument of $q$, and denoted by $\mathrm{Arg}(q)$: hence
> $$L (q, p) := \log |q| + \mathrm{Arg}(q).$$
> Indeed, if $(q,p)\in \ {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$, then $q=r\exp p$ for $r=|q|$ and $L$ can be rewritten as
> $$L (r\exp p, p) = \log r + p.$$

### Proposition 5.4 (Section 5; printed p. 989; ar5iv id `S5.Thmtheo4`)
> **Proposition 5.4**
> The map
> $$L : {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+ \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$$
> is the inverse of the ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$-exponential $E$, and a diffeomorphism from ${\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$ to ${\mathbb {K}}$.

### Definition 5.5 (Section 5; printed p. 989, running onto p. 990; ar5iv id `S5.Thmtheo5`)
> **Definition 5.5**
> Let $\pi : {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+ \subset {\mathbb {K}}\times \mathrm{Im}({\mathbb {K}}) \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}\setminus \{0\}$ denote the natural projection
> $$(q, p) \mapsto q$$
> and let $\Omega \subset {\mathscr {E}}_{\mathbb {K}}^+$ be a path connected subset such that $\pi _{|_{\Omega }}$ is injective. Then, the map
> $$\log _{\mathbb {K}}: \pi (\Omega ) \rightarrow {\mathbb {K}}$$
> defined by
> $$\log _{\mathbb {K}}q = L( \pi _{|_{\Omega }}^{-1}(q) )$$
> is called a branch or a determination of the hypercomplex logarithm on $\pi (\Omega )$.

The paragraph immediately following Definition 5.5 (published text; outside the definition environment):
> Notice that, as expected, with the notations of Definition 5.5 we have that
> $$\exp (\log _{\mathbb {K}}q)= \pi (E (L( \pi _{|_{\Omega }}^{-1}(q) )))=\pi (\pi _{|_{\Omega }}^{-1}(q) )=q$$
> for all $q$ in $\pi (\Omega )$.

### Supporting: declaration of the symbols K and S (Section 1, "Preface"; printed p. 972)
> Let ${\mathbb {K}}$ be either the division algebra of quaternions ${\mathbb {H}}$ or the division algebra of octonions ${\mathbb {O}}$; we denote by $\dim {\mathbb {K}}$ the real dimension of ${\mathbb {K}}$, namely $\dim {\mathbb {H}}=4$ and $\dim {\mathbb {O}}=8$. Let ${\mathbb {S}}_{{\mathbb {K}}}\subset {\mathbb {K}}$ be the 2-sphere or, respectively, the 6-sphere of imaginary units, i.e. the sets of $I\in {\mathbb {K}}$ such that $I^2=-1$. For the sake of simplicity, both in the case of quaternions and in the case of octonions we will simply write ${\mathbb {S}}$ instead of ${\mathbb {S}}_{{\mathbb {K}}}$ since no confusion can arise.

### Supporting: the Preface passage printing a degenerate-fibre formula (Section 1, "Preface"; printed p. 972)
> The construction of the logarithm and its branches given in the complex case cannot be directly replicated in the quaternionic and octonionic environments. This is mainly due to the fact that the exponential function
> $$\exp q=\sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{q^n}{n!}$$
> is an entire function (i.e., its domain of definition is ${\mathbb {K}}$), but cannot be used to define a covering of ${\mathbb {K}}\setminus \{0\}$. In fact, for all $0 \ne x\in {\mathbb {R}}$, the preimage of $x$ is not a discrete set but consists of infinitely many 2 or 6 dimensional spheres. Indeed, for instance in the case $x<0$, setting ${\mathbb {S}}(2k+1)\pi =\{q(2k+1)\pi : q\in {\mathbb {S}}\}$, we have
> $$(\exp )^{-1}(x)=\{ \log |x| + {\mathbb {S}}(2k+1)\pi : k\in {\mathbb {Z}}\}.$$
> It follows that, contrarily to what happens in the case of the complex logarithm, no continuous branch of the quaternionic or octonionic logarithm can be defined on any open neighborhood of any strictly negative $x\in {\mathbb {R}}$.
